\section{Numerical integration and experimental settings}\label{app:computation}

The population evidence requires integration over unobserved characteristics. The following checks assess sensitivity to the numerical approximation, while the fitting settings specify the models whose evidence and attributions are compared.

The latent posterior $p(s,m,q\mid x_S)$ is evaluated on a grid of $34^3$ points spanning $\pm4.3$ standard deviations, and the integral is computed by drawing 8{,}192 latent values from the grid posterior (with uniform jitter inside each cell) and one draw of the absent blocks for each. Two checks support the oracle. The probability integral transform of the true latent values under the grid posterior has mean between 0.47 and 0.51 and standard deviation between 0.28 and 0.30 for each factor, close to the uniform values of 0.5 and 0.289. A logistic regression of fresh outcomes on the oracle $\logit\Prb(Y=1\mid x_{-j})$ has slope between 0.98 and 1.01 for every block. The same draws give the class-level oracle $W^B_j$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:binwoe}, computed from the Monte Carlo frequencies of the applicant's class among the draws, and the booked-population oracle of Eq.~\eqref{eq:booked}.

These checks do not bound the error for an individual applicant on the logit scale, so parts of the oracle were recomputed for replicate 1. The leave-one-block-out and class-level evidence were recomputed for 300 random test applicants outside the declined sample, using both independent draws at the original settings and 32{,}768 draws on a $48^3$ grid spanning $\pm5$ standard deviations. Coalition evidence was recomputed for 100 declined applicants using the finer settings. With the finer computation, the root mean squared change in $W_j$ is between 0.001 and 0.011 logit units across blocks and the 99th percentile of the absolute change is at most 0.030; for $W^B_j$ the corresponding values are at most 0.011 and 0.029, and for the coalition evidence $E_j$ at most 0.005 and 0.014. The independent replicate at the original settings changes $W_j$ by at most 0.015 in root mean square. These changes are small relative to the estimator errors in the main results, which are at least 0.05. The smallest Monte Carlo probability of an applicant's class among these applicants is $4.9\times10^{-4}$, about four draws, and no class probability was zero. Across all saved oracle values, the conditional default probabilities lie between 0.004 and 0.9995 for the leave-one-block-out calculation and between 0.009 and 0.9995 for the coalition calculation, so clipping at $10^{-4}$ and $1-10^{-4}$ is not active for them. These are discrepancies between numerical calculations on the checked subsets, not bounds on the error relative to the exact integrals, and an approximation bias shared by both calculations would not be detected. The oracle values are numerical estimates of the population quantities.

The primary risk, projection and neighborhood models use XGBoost \citep{xgboost} using trees of depth 4, learning rate 0.05, row subsampling 0.8 and early stopping on a random 20 percent of its training sample. The multiclass geometry models use learning rate 0.1. The kernel exponent is chosen from $\{1,2,4,8,16,32\}$ and the smoothing constant from $\{0.1,0.5,2,8,32\}$. Each block has five classes except delinquency, which has three because most records have no delinquent account, and public record, which has two. Each declined applicant is compared with 50 references. A separate run for replicate 1 recomputes the leaf-local estimates with evidence samples of 2{,}500, 10{,}000, 30{,}000 and 100{,}000 records drawn from the same evidence sample, with the geometry models and calibration records unchanged.
